You've heard "AI agent" everywhere. Here's the calm version, no jargon.

## A chatbot answers. An agent acts.

A normal chatbot waits for you, replies, and stops. An **agent** is given a goal
and then takes steps on its own to reach it: it can look things up, use tools,
check its own work, and try again if something fails.

Think of the difference between asking a friend "how do I book a flight?" versus
handing them your card and saying "book me the cheapest flight on Friday." The
second one is acting on a goal.

## What makes something an agent

- **A goal**, not just a question.
- **Tools** it can use (search, a calculator, your calendar, code).
- **Memory** of what it already did.
- **A loop**: try, check the result, adjust, repeat until done.

## Where you'll actually meet them

You don't need to build one to use one. Agents already power things like coding
assistants that edit files for you, research helpers that read ten pages and
summarize, and inbox tools that draft replies.

> You stay in charge. A good agent shows its steps and asks before doing anything
> risky.

## The one-line takeaway

An AI agent is just AI with a goal and the tools to chase it — step by step,
with you watching.
