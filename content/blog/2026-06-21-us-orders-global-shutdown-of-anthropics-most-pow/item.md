On June 12, 2026, the U.S. government ordered Anthropic to suspend all access to its two most advanced artificial intelligence models: Claude Fable 5 (also known as Fable 5) and Claude Mythos 5 (or Mythos 5).

The order was issued as an export control directive, invoking national security authorities. The mandate requires that all foreign nationals—regardless of whether they are inside or outside the U.S., and even foreign employees of Anthropic—lose access to Fable 5 and Mythos 5.

Because it is virtually impossible to block only foreign users without affecting U.S. users, Anthropic deactivated both models for all customers worldwide. The shutdown affects only Fable 5 and Mythos 5; all other Anthropic models continue to operate normally.

Why the Government Says It Did It
The government did not publicly specify the technical details of its concern, but according to sources cited by Semafor and Anthropic's statement:

The government believes it has discovered a method to jailbreak Fable 5's safeguards, which would allow its security limitations to be bypassed.

There is a suspicion that a group linked to China may have gained access to the capabilities of Mythos and Fable 5, which is considered a risk to U.S. national security.

The Trump administration also fears that China could use the model for reverse engineering and "distillation," thereby copying its advanced cybersecurity capabilities.

The government considers it a risk that Mythos's capabilities could fall into the hands of foreign actors, including the Chinese government.

In short: the U.S. government sees these models as a potential advanced cybersecurity tool that, if it falls into the hands of adversaries (especially China), could be used for autonomous attacks, reverse engineering, and copying critical capabilities.

What Anthropic Says
Anthropic complied with the order and deactivated Fable 5 and Mythos 5, but in its official statement, it expressed strong disagreement with the way the government handled the situation:

They were not given technical details: the letter does not specify the concrete reason for the national security concern.

Anthropic believes the government relied on a “narrow, not universal” jailbreak, which simply allows the model to read code and fix bugs.

The company reviewed the demo and found that it only exposes minor and previously known vulnerabilities, which other public models can also discover without needing a jailbreak.

Anthropic maintains that its “defense in depth” strategy with Fable 5:

Has strong safeguards that greatly reduce the risk of cybersecurity misuse.

Was red-teamed for thousands of hours with the US government, the UK's AISI, and third parties.

It is more effective than any previously deployed model.

It has not found a universal jailbreak (one that widely bypasses the safeguards).

The company has not received any reports of a concerning jailbreak that has caused actual harm; the findings are benign responses or minor findings without significant impact.

Anthropic argues that if this standard were applied industry-wide, almost all deployments of new Frontier models would be halted.

The company says the government should be able to block insecure deployments, but through a transparent, equitable, clear, and technically sound regulatory process; and that this action does not align with those principles.

Anthropic describes it as a “misunderstanding” and expresses its desire to restore access “as soon as possible.”

Two Key Comments
1. On the US Government's Stance
The US government is acting from a national security and geopolitical perspective:

It views Fable 5 and Mythos 5 as possessing such advanced cybersecurity capabilities that, if an adversary (especially China) were to acquire them, it could:

Carry out autonomous attacks against critical infrastructure.

Use reverse engineering and distillation to copy capabilities and compete technologically.

Faced with the lack of a clear and technical regulatory process, the government is opting for a drastic and rapid measure: export controls that block access for all foreigners, which in practice amounts to a global blackout.

This reflects a trend of containing the most advanced AI technology as a strategic asset, similar to what is done with chips, cryptography, or dual-use technologies.

The risk is that, being a very broad measure without publicly available technical details, it will be perceived as excessive and generate uncertainty in the industry.

2. Regarding Anthropic's stance
Anthropic adopts the stance of a technical company defending its model and security strategy:

It argues that the real problem is a limited jailbreak, not a systemic flaw, and that the exposed capabilities are already available in other models (including GPT-5.5) and are used by security advocates.

Critique Anthropic is blocking a business model used by hundreds of millions of people because of a finding that, according to them, hasn't caused any real harm.

See this decision as a dangerous precedent: if this standard is applied to the entire industry, it would stifle innovation and the deployment of new, cutting-edge models.

Anthropic is calling for a transparent regulatory process based on technical facts, not sweeping, secret orders that affect the entire market.

In short: the US government prioritizes national security and geopolitical control of very powerful AI, while Anthropic argues that its model is safe and well-protected, and that the measure is excessive and poorly justified, potentially stifling AI innovation if it becomes widespread.
